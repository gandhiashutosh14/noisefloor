## What the results say

1. **The primary hypothesis holds.** With 80% of telemetry variance as unpredictable noise, the
   JEPA agent's daily cost is 38.5 lower than the reconstruction agent's (281.5 vs 320.0, 12%;
   bootstrap 95% CI [-56.6, -21.4], seed t-interval [-56.2, -20.8]; 8 seeds x 32 paired days).
   With no noise the two are equivalent within 3%. With noise JEPA is ahead at every level tested:
   -40.9 at 50%, -38.5 at 80% and -132.7 at 95%, where reconstruction degrades to 407 and JEPA only
   to 275. It also holds when the noise is spread over all 128 directions (-21.5).

2. **JEPA on noisy telemetry ties a tuned runbook on clean metrics (H2 not supported).** 281.5 vs
   285.3, interval [-32.0, 25.7]. It gets there differently: 26% fewer replica-hours per day (309 vs
   418) and more SLO-violation minutes (85 vs 23). On unseen load shapes it does better than the
   runbook (270.1 vs 341.0; 84% of the runbook-to-oracle gap closed, reconstruction 68%), but OOD
   was exploratory and not tested.

3. **SIGReg is not the best way to stop collapse here (two secondary results reversed).** A JEPA
   with an EMA teacher (263.4) and a model whose encoder is shaped only by the violation-risk loss
   (242.2, 75% of the gap closed) both plan better than JEPA-SIGReg at d = 0.8. The probes show
   why: SIGReg pushes every direction of the 16-dimensional latent toward a unit Gaussian, so the
   latent uses all of it (effective rank 15.7), and whatever is not needed for the state fills with
   noise (distractor R^2 0.43). The EMA model settles on a low-rank code (rank 6.9) that carries more
   of the state (R^2 0.68) and almost none of the noise (0.045). SIGReg's guarantee against collapse
   costs, in this regime, the ability to throw information away.

4. **Collapse control (H3 fails on its lower half).** Without SIGReg (lambda = 0) the latent does
   shrink, to rank 5.5-6.2, not to the pre-registered <= 3: the inverse-dynamics and risk heads
   keep it from collapsing fully. That model is the worst learned agent (452.6); the EMA teacher,
   which also yields a low-rank code, is among the best, so low rank alone is not the problem.

5. **The falsification arm did not behave as predicted.** When the distractors are predictable, a
   predictive objective has reason to encode them, so JEPA's edge should shrink. The latent does
   encode them (distractor R^2 0.74), but reconstruction suffers more (376.0 vs 301.4), and the
   difference in differences (-27.0, interval [-62.1, 2.5]) is not distinguishable from zero.

**The latent-width ablation qualifies H1.** Reconstruction's failure at width 16 is a bottleneck
effect: 16 loud distractor sources compete with the state for 16 dimensions. With 32 dimensions
there is room for both, and reconstruction (266.0) plans better than any JEPA-SIGReg width tried.
JEPA-SIGReg, forced to use every dimension it has, puts the extra room into noise (rank 28,
distractor R^2 0.73). The pre-registered result stands for the registered width; it should not be
read as "JEPA beats reconstruction" in general.

## Planning quality

At d = 0.8 the realised 8-step cost was 1.15x what JEPA's planner expected (reconstruction 1.25x,
lambda = 0 1.65x), under the 1.5 threshold the design set for "the planner exploits model error".
A planning step (CEM, 128 samples, 3 iterations, 8-step horizon, 16 environments) took 2.9 ms per
environment on the CI runner's CPU.
